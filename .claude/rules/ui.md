# What the workspace draws and how it is driven

**Messages, menus and questions.** What a key or command has to say goes through `Server::say` (`crates/ui/src/message.rs`; `switchboard say` from a script), which shows it in a box in the middle of the screen, or, with Settings' `message_boxes` off, as a line in the status bar; nothing says anything another way. Every menu, question and list looks one way, mission control's: a box drawn by `crates/ui/src/draw.rs`'s `draw_box`, the chosen option on the highlight with a yellow `›`, the keys dim on a row of their own (but M-F's search box, which board 78 draws with no keys, so its results get the room); a new one uses `src/ui/`'s menu, question and picker, so `/` searches it as it searches every menu (a box with a list of its own, such as M-m's, the answer box and Notes' Format menu, takes `ui::menu::Filter`). What a part draws with UX 2.0 on (a state's mark, a section's label, a chip, a stage's cell, a key and what it does, a card, a list's chosen row, the keys on a footer or bar) comes from `crates/ui/src/kit.rs`'s atoms rather than spans of its own, so one state has one mark and one color everywhere. A row is laid out with `kit::columns` (fixed, stretching and right-edge columns, dropped in the view's order), never at a board's own offsets, so a view fills any width as UX 2.0 board 45 says. Anything that takes a choice takes a click too (`menu_mouse`), lights under the mouse (`hover_highlight`), is reached by Tab (`tab_focus`: in mission control Tab moves a section at a time, wrapping, through `Mission::sections` and the view's panes, a section's rows reached with `j` and `k`), and gets easy mode's × (`easy_keys`), as the boxes there do. A notification's menu is its item's menu in the view it came from, built by `choices::notice_choices` from that view's `item_choices` with dismissing added, so a new kind of notification names its view in `notices::view_of` (or, when no view lists it as an item, goes in `notices::NO_VIEW` with what it leads to in `choices::notice_leads`) and lists no choices of its own, and a choice added to a view's menu reaches its notifications by itself. Every yes/no question takes Enter as yes and shows `[Y/n]`, and a closed input is always no, so nothing piped or unattended says yes. A pane beside a board scrolls with `J` and `K` and the wheel over it, and resizes with the pane resize keys and a drag of its edge, through `About::window` and `side_width` (`src/mission/render.rs`), so a new pane does too. A sidebar resizes the same way while Settings' `sidebar_resize` is on, by a drag of its edge and the pane resize keys while it has the keys, its width kept and "Use the default width" in its menu clearing it: the workspace's, with mission control's list of views in its place (`Session::sidebar_width`, saved with the layout). Text is measured with `ui::draw::width` (or `sidebar::text::width`), which counts what a terminal draws as two columns as two; one counted short shifts every border after it.

**Keyboard first, mouse complete.** Everything the mouse does, a key does too, a menu a right click opens included; nothing is the mouse's alone. Everything a key does, a click does too, on something drawn that lights under the mouse and is reached by Tab. In every mission control view, Enter (and Space, and a click on an item) opens the chosen item's menu, what Enter did by itself before being its first option, chosen as it opens, and `m` opens the view's own menu; a view has no keys of its own that act on an item, only moving (`j`/`k`, `h`/`l`, `H`/`L`, Tab, and `C-w` and a direction between a view's panes, as Vim's windows), `/` to filter and mission control's own keys stay keys, and an action is an option of a menu. A right click on an item opens what Enter opens, on a view's background what `m` opens; `m` in the sidebar and `S-F10` anywhere open the menu a right click on what is chosen would. A menu or box a click opens appears beside the click (`Place::Near`), one a key opens in the middle, or, for a box about the tab in front (`Server::about_tab`: its links, renaming it, answering its Claude, its menu, `M-m`'s PR readiness), as a pane in the empty columns beside a narrow lone pane when it fits there (`Look::middle`). Every mission control view is listed in `M-p`, and every action a view has is an option of one of its menus.

**One look per thing.** A card, a board of columns, a person's avatar, a chip, a meter or a mark is drawn by its one atom in `crates/ui/src/kit.rs` (`kit::board`, `kit::avatar` and the rest), never by a view's copy of it, so Slack's, Linear's and GitHub's people look alike and every board the same. The pane with the keys is drawn at full strength and every other pane greyed, through `kit::unfocused` over the pane's cells once it is drawn, the look a tab's pane without the focus has: mission control's panes and its list of views (`Mission::without_keys`, which finds a view's panes by the hit boxes it drew, a pane beside by its `Spot::Side` rows, and among several views at once the views without the focus by their `Spot::Cell` rows), Notes' list, note and plan, and the diff's list; a new view with panes does the same, and gives a pane beside its `Spot::Side` rows. The one exception is a pane that shows what the pane with the keys has chosen, a list's preview, which is not greyed, since it is what is being read from the list: the diff's file beside its list, so the diff greys nothing while its list has the keys and only the list while the file does; a mission control view's pane beside while its list has the keys and its list while the panel over it (Settings' categories, Slack's sections) does (`Mission::previews`); and Notes' plan while the note has the keys and its cursor is in the plan's item. A change to what something looks like is drawn first as a board in `docs/design/src/` (the UX 2.0 canvas, where the boards started, is kept as a read-only archive), published with `/design-docs` and reviewed on the site, and its item waits under its plan's "Decision needed" until the drawing is approved; the plan names the boards it follows by number, in that option too, so autopilot holds the item, and hands-free never decides it (`Plan::Review`).

**Tips.** A tip is its text in `src/ui/tip.rs`'s `text_of` and a trigger calling `Server::tip`, shown once each in the top-right corner, its key passed on to what was under it; never in the status line, where one that goes on the next key press is missed. Doing what a tip teaches marks it shown.

**Keys and the palette.** A new key is an `Action` in `src/mux/keys.rs` (or a part's `Command`, with its `key()`), with `about()` and `tags()`: the palette lists every action by them, and an action no key is bound to by default is in `Action::palette_only`, listed with no key. The defaults are the keys tmux.conf bound. A new binding's tags say what it is about (`#git`, `#pr`, `#tabs`), and one that uses GitHub or Linear carries that service's tag.

**Tab names and colors.** A tab's hue is its own, chosen as it opens to stand apart from the open tabs' (in one of two lightness steps) and kept with the layout (`@tab_hue`, `@tab_step`), and `switchboard statusline` asks the server for it, so Claude's statusline and the tab agree; the hash of a key `src/tabs/color.rs` builds is only the tie-break, a lone tab's color, the color with Settings' `tab_colors_apart` off, and the statusline's outside the workspace; Settings' `tab_colors` picks how the hue is drawn (soft or vivid), never the key or the hue; the key's shape stays byte for byte as it is, since every project's color hangs on it, while the name the tab shows is free to change. The `#<number>` at the front of a tab's label is its PR, which `wt-jump` reads back, so it stays at the front.
